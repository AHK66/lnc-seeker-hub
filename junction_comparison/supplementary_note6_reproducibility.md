## 6.4.8 Reproducibility of tumor-specific junctions across independent cohorts

To assess whether tumor-specific junctions represent a reproducible biological signal
rather than cohort-specific sequencing or alignment artifacts, we quantified their
reproducibility in an independent cohort of the same cancer type. Tumor-specific
junctions were defined as those detected in the TCGA-BRCA tumor cohort but not in the
matched TCGA-BRCA normal cohort (across the six analyzed loci, at each read-support
threshold). These junctions were then tested for independent detection in the GSE235167
PDX breast-cancer cohort, which is fully independent of TCGA and was sequenced on a
different platform (Illumina HiSeq 4000), with a different aligner and a different read
length (approximately 300 bp versus approximately 50 bp). Because a splice junction is
identified by its genomic coordinates, recovery of a TCGA tumor-specific junction in the
PDX cohort constitutes a cross-cohort detection that is independent of the originating
platform and aligner.

At a minimum read-support threshold of 1, 54 of 137 tumor-specific junctions (39.4%)
were independently detected in the PDX cohort; the rate was 46.2% (threshold 2), 34.1%
(threshold 5), and 33.3% (threshold 10) (Supplementary Table 6.4). Restricting the
analysis to the novel (unannotated) tumor-specific junctions — the subset not represented
in either RefSeq or GENCODE lncRNA v48 — yielded a comparable reproducibility (48/129 =
37.2% at threshold 1; 26.2% at threshold 10), indicating that the novel junctions are not
disproportionately likely to be technical artifacts.

As a tissue-specificity control, the tumor-specific junctions were also compared against
the unrelated TCGA-PRAD (prostate) cohort. Approximately 20–24% of tumor-specific
junctions were additionally detected in prostate, indicating that a fraction are broadly
expressed (e.g., housekeeping) junctions that were under-sampled in the matched normal set
rather than specific to breast tumor. Excluding these, the stricter set of junctions absent
in both the matched normal and the prostate cohort (breast-tumor-specific) was reproduced
in the PDX cohort in 24.5–36.7% of cases across thresholds.

Per-locus, reproducibility was highest for GAPDH (38.5–64.7%) and CD44 (33.3–56.4%) and
lower for the highly expressed housekeeping locus ACTB (0–9.1%), which is expected given
the low proportion of tumor-specific junctions at that locus (Supplementary Table 6.5). As
a built-in negative control, PCA3 yielded no tumor-specific junctions in the breast
cohorts, consistent with its prostate-specific expression.

Together, these results indicate that a substantial and consistent fraction of
tumor-specific — including novel — splice junctions are reproducibly detected across
independent breast-cancer cohorts sequenced on different platforms and with different
aligners, supporting their interpretation as reproducible biological signals rather than
cohort-specific sequencing or alignment artifacts.

## Methods (append to §6.6 Methods Summary)

**Reproducibility of tumor-specific junctions.** Tumor-specific junctions were defined as
the set difference `J_LSH(BRCA tumor) \ J_LSH(BRCA normal)` at each read-support
threshold, using the cohort-aggregated junction sets restricted to the six analyzed loci
(region-filtered, unstranded junction identity `chrom:start-end`). Reproducibility was
measured as the fraction of these junctions also present in the independent GSE235167 PDX
cohort junction set `J_LSH(PDX)`. A tissue-specificity control was applied against the
unrelated TCGA-PRAD (prostate) cohort; broadly expressed junctions (those also detected in
PRAD) were separately excluded to define a stricter breast-tumor-specific subset. Novel
junctions were those absent from both the RefSeq and GENCODE lncRNA v48 junction sets. The
analysis is implemented in `junction_comparison/tumor_specific_reproducibility.py`, reuses
the junction definition of the main comparison, and verifies against the published totals
of Table 6.3 before reporting.

## Limitations (append to §6.5 Limitations)

**Cross-cohort reproducibility.** The reproducibility assessment compares the TCGA-BRCA
tumor cohort (approximately 50 bp, GDC-realigned) with the GSE235167 PDX cohort
(approximately 300 bp, independently aligned). The two cohorts differ in platform, read
length, alignment, and in that the PDX models are derived from — but not identical to — the
original tumors and contain a murine stromal component. Reproducibility is therefore
assessed at the level of independently detected junction coordinates rather than as an exact
concordance, and a junction present in only one of the two cohorts cannot be distinguished
here between genuine cohort-specific biology and differential detection sensitivity.
